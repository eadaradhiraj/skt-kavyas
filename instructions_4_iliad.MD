### **1. Structural & Layout Requirements**
1. **Verse Number:** Always place the verse number (e.g., **1.130**) at the very top of every response.
2. **Greek Romanised:** Always include the Romanised Greek text of the verse.
3. **English Meaning:** Always include the English translation directly below the Romanised Greek.
4. **Sanskrit Prose (*Gadhya*):** Include a prose sentence in both Devanagari and Harvard-Kyoto (HK).
5. **Sanskrit Verse (*Anuṣṭubh*):** Include the 16-syllable Śloka in both Harvard-Kyoto (HK) and Devanagari.
6. **Brief Explanation:** Provide a short explanation of the grammatical and translation choices.
7. **Wrappable Syllable Breakdown:** Provide the syllable-by-syllable weight breakdown using plain-text slash format (`1. ka (L) / 2. thaM (G) ...`). **Never use Markdown tables**, as they break screen wrapping.

### **2. Meter Rules (*Anuṣṭubh / Śloka*)**
1. **Syllable Count:** The verse must contain exactly 16 syllables, split into two 8-syllable Pādas (8 + 8).
2. **Pāda 1 Cadence:** Syllables 5, 6, and 7 **must strictly be L-G-G** (Laghu, Guru, Guru).
3. **Pāda 2 Cadence:** Syllables 13, 14, and 15 (which are 5, 6, 7 of Pāda 2) **must strictly be L-G-L** (Laghu, Guru, Laghu).
4. **Metrical Precision:** Syllable weights (*Mātrā*) must be calculated with 100% precision across conjuncts and word boundaries.

---

### **3. Pāṇinian Grammar & Vocabulary Rules**
1. **Top Priority:** Pāṇinian Vyākaraṇa takes absolute priority over matching Greek syntax or forcing a meter.
2. **No *Ktavatu*:** Avoid the *ktavatu* past active participle (e.g., *uktavān*, *pūritavān*); prefer finite verbs (imperfect *laṅ*, aorist *luṅ*, perfect *liṭ*, future *lṛt*) or gerunds.
3. **Subject Continuity (*Samānakartṛka*):** Per Pāṇini 3.4.21 (*samānakartṛkayoḥ pūrvakāle*), gerunds (*ktvā/lyap*) must share the exact same subject as the main verb. If the subject changes between clauses, use finite verbs to prevent declension/subject mismatch.
4. **No Extra Words:** Do not add arbitrary filler words or concepts that do not exist in the original Greek text.

---

### **4. Sandhi Rules**
1. **Visarga before Unvoiced Sibilants (*Vā Śari*, 8.3.36):** Always keep the visarga (`H`) before unvoiced sibilants (`s`, `z`, `S`) instead of assimilating it (e.g., write `saH saMrambhAt` rather than `sas saMrambhAt`).
2. **Mandatory Sandhi:** Rules like *sulopo 'kor anañsamāse hali* (6.1.132 — dropping visarga of *saH* before consonants $\rightarrow$ *sa*) must be strictly applied in the verse.

### **5. Rules for Names & Epithets**
1. **Names in Verse:** Translate Greek proper names etymologically using Sanskrit roots or Indo-European cognates:
   * **Agamemnon:** *atimantA* (अतिमन्ता — "great thinker/counselor").
   * **Achilles:** *lokAghaH* (लोकाघः) / *senAghaH* (सेनाघः) ("grief to the people/army").
   * **Calchas:** *cintakaH* (चिन्तकः — "the ponderer").
   * **Zeus:** *Dyaus* (द्यौः) / *divaspriya* (cognate with PIE *\*dyēws*).
   * **Apollo:** *rujAharaH* (रुजाहरः — "disease-destroyer").
   * **Clytemnestra:** *zrutakIrtiH* (श्रुतकीर्तिः) (using the PIE cognate *śruta* for *klutós*).
   * **Chryseis:** *haimaputrI* (हैमपुत्री — "daughter of Chryses/Golden").
   * **Ilium/Troy:** *varaNapuraM* (वरणपुरम् — "enclosed city", from PIE *\*wel-*).
   * **Swift-footed:** *zIghrapAt* (शीघ्रपात्) / *zIghrAGghriH* (शीघ्राङ्घ्रिः).
2. **Prose (*Gadhya*) Rules:**
   * **No Speaker Prefix for Narrator:** Do not add headers like *kaviH* for narrative lines.
   * **Character Names:** In the prose text, use transliterated Greek names (e.g., *agamemnonena*, *akhilIsaH*) alongside descriptive terms to avoid vague pronouns.
   * **No Sandhi in Prose:** Keep all Sanskrit words in the Prose section completely un-sandhied in their standalone dictionary forms for maximum clarity.
